Exponential and mixture families, by hand

Companion to the notes on Chapter 2 of Amari's Information Geometry and Its Applications. Back to the notes. Each widget below is one idea of the chapter made touchable: $\psi$ as a cumulant function, the two kinds of mixing, maximum entropy, independence, maximum likelihood as a projection, the kernel exponential family, and the dictionary between Bregman divergences and exponential families.

1. $\psi$ as the cumulant function: slope is the mean, curvature is the variance

An exponential family is $p(x;\theta)=\exp\{\theta x-\psi(\theta)\}$ with respect to a fixed reference measure. Move $\theta_0$. The tangent slope of $\psi$ at $\theta_0$ is the mean $\eta=\psi'(\theta_0)$, and its curvature $\psi''(\theta_0)$ is the variance, which is the Fisher information. The right plot is the distribution itself, with the mean marked and a bar of width one standard deviation $\sqrt{\psi''}$.

2. Two kinds of mixing: geometric and arithmetic

Join $p=N(-d/2,\sigma_p^2)$ to $q=N(d/2,\sigma_q^2)$. The m-geodesic is the arithmetic mixture $(1-t)p+tq$; the e-geodesic is the geometric mixture $\propto p^{1-t}q^{t}$, a one-parameter exponential family with natural parameter $t$. Pull the means apart and the arithmetic mixture grows two humps; the geometric one stays a single bell. Make the widths differ and the arithmetic mixture grows heavy tails while the geometric one stays Gaussian.

3. Maximum entropy: an exponential tilt of the uniform

Among all distributions of a die with mean $a$, which has the largest entropy? The family of such distributions is m-flat; the maximiser is the e-projection of the uniform distribution onto it, an exponential tilt $p(x)\propto e^{\theta x}$. Slide $a$, then draw a random die with the same mean: its entropy is lower, and $\mathrm{KL}[P{:}P_0]=\mathrm{KL}[P{:}\hat P]+\mathrm{KL}[\hat P{:}P_0]$ holds exactly. The parameter $\theta$ that comes out is not equal to $a$: the constraint fixes the expectation parameter, not the natural one.

4. Independence is e-flat and not m-flat; mutual information is the m-distance to it

Top: a 2×2 table $P$ built from its marginals and its odds ratio. The closest independent table in KL is the product of the marginals, and the distance is the mutual information. Bottom: take two independent tables and mix them. The geometric mixture is independent (odds ratio exactly 1), so independence is e-flat; the arithmetic mixture is not, so independence is not m-flat.

5. Maximum likelihood is an m-projection (Hardy–Weinberg)

Genotypes AA, Aa, aa occur with frequencies $(q^2,\,2q(1-q),\,(1-q)^2)$ when the allele A has frequency $q$. In the probability triangle this is a curve, and in the natural coordinates it is a straight line, so the model is e-flat. Set the observed counts. The observed frequencies $\bar p$ project onto the curve along a straight segment (an m-geodesic), landing at the maximum likelihood estimate $\hat q=(2n_{AA}+n_{Aa})/(2N)$. The right plot shows $\mathrm{KL}[\bar p{:}p(q)]$ and the split $\mathrm{KL}[\bar p{:}\hat p]+\mathrm{KL}[\hat p{:}p(q)]$ lying on top of each other.

6. The kernel exponential family

Replace the finitely many statistics $x_i$ by a whole family of them, one per point $y$: $p(x)=\exp\{\sum_j\alpha_j\,k(x,y_j)-\psi(\alpha)\}$ with respect to a base measure $\mu$. With three centres $y=(-1,\,0.5,\,2)$ and a Gaussian kernel of width $\sigma$, the weights $\alpha_j$ are the natural parameters, and the dual parameter is the mean embedding $\eta(y)=\mathbb E[k(x,y)]$. Positive weights add bumps, negative weights dig holes; $\psi$ stays convex in $\alpha$ because its Hessian is a covariance.

7. A Bregman divergence is the KL divergence of an exponential family

Choose a convex $\psi$. There is an exponential family whose cumulant function is $\psi$ (Theorem 2.2), and $D_\psi[\theta{:}\theta']=\mathrm{KL}[p_{\theta'}{:}p_\theta]$. The three examples: $\psi=\theta^2/2$ gives the Gaussians $N(\theta,1)$ and squared Euclidean distance; $\psi=e^\theta$ gives the Poissons and the generalised KL; $\psi=-\log(-\theta)$ gives the exponential distributions and the Itakura–Saito divergence. The readout compares the formula with a direct sum or integral.

8. The Pythagorean identity in function space: the gap is the pairing

For three distributions $p,q,r$ the exact identity is $\mathrm{KL}[p{:}r]-\mathrm{KL}[p{:}q]-\mathrm{KL}[q{:}r]=-\sum(p-q)(\log q-\log r)$, the pairing of the mixture direction $p-q$ with the exponential direction $\log r-\log q$. Here $r\propto q\,e^{t\,w}$ moves along an e-geodesic from $q$; tilt $w$ from perpendicular to the pairing and the gap is exactly the pairing. At tilt zero it is zero, whatever $t$ is.
